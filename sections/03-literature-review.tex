\section{A consistent review structure}
For each paper, explain the problem, the program and machine assumptions, the
analysis or cost model, the transformation strategy, and the evaluation evidence.
Distinguish the authors' claims from your own interpretation.
\citet{carrMckinleyTseng1994} is an additional starting point from the reading list.

\subsection{Paper review: Wolf and Lam (1991)}
\begin{description}
  \item[Problem.] \todo{State the locality problem the paper addresses.}

  \item[Algorithm.] \todo{Explain the transformation choices and their ordering.}
  \item[Evidence.] \todo{Identify benchmarks, hardware, baselines, and results.}
  \item[Limitations.] \todo{Discuss assumptions and cases outside the model.}
\end{description}
For a specific claim, provide a pinpoint citation, for example:
\citep[Section~2]{wolfLam1991locality}. Check the section or page number in the
paper before using it in your final prose.
